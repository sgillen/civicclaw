# SF Civic Digest — Week of July 13, 2026
*District 9 · Mission / Bernal Heights / Portola*

---

## TLDR

- A 379-unit mixed-income proposal at 3350 Mission Street entered review, keeping the Mission corridor in the middle of the district's biggest housing fight this week.
- District 9 still has approvals on paper, but the gap between entitlement and construction is visible: 2675 Folsom has an approval letter, while 2588 Mission and 222 Capp remain stuck.
- Two live planning notices are open now, at 2595 Folsom Street and 212 Elsie Street/313 Winfield Street, giving neighbors a direct comment window before later hearings.
- Supervisor Jackie Fielder put Mission Street BART plazas on the July 14 Board agenda, which makes plaza safety and operations one of the few clearly district-specific City Hall topics this week.
- Small private permits dominated the permit feed, but they clustered in real reinvestment rather than cosmetic work, including a grocery kitchen buildout at 2934 24th Street and several larger home expansions.
- District 9 has one of the busiest volunteer calendars in the city right now, with repeated cleanups across the Mission, Bernal Heights, Portola, and nearby corridors over the next two weeks.

## Housing and Development

District 9 had the busiest housing pipeline of these three districts. The biggest live item is [3350 Mission Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2026-005139PRJ), a 379-unit mixed-income proposal now under review, while [3230 24th Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2026-005716PRJ) adds a smaller 20-unit project on the same streamlined track.

The bigger story is what happens after approval. [2675 Folsom Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2024-001476PRJ) now has an approval letter, which is meaningful, but [2588 Mission Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2018-013877PRJ) remains on hold and [1458 San Bruno Avenue](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2019-022404PRJ) has been under review since 2019. That fits the citywide pattern from `sf-build-timelines.md`: approvals are common, financing and permit follow-through are the harder step.

## Permits and Corridor Activity

The permit feed was mostly private-property reinvestment, not new towers. The clearest commercial item was a kitchen buildout at `2934 24th Street`, while larger residential remodels landed at `913 Florida Street`, `170 Bocana Street`, and `546 Ellsworth Street`.

That mix usually means the district is still seeing household-level spending even when larger housing projects are stuck in the approval-to-permit dead zone. It is movement, but not the kind that changes overall housing supply quickly.

## Citywide

City Hall is crowded this week. The Board and its committees are moving hotel-use rules in Mission-adjacent zoning, broader planning code changes, campaign-finance updates, and a drug-free supportive-housing ordinance, so the citywide policy backdrop is still mostly about how San Francisco wants growth, public order, and oversight to work.

Housing citywide still looks like District 9 scaled up: lots of entitled or newly approved projects, much less evidence of construction starting fast. That is the same approval-versus-financing gap the build-timeline reference warns about, not a District 9-only problem.

On ethics and money, the biggest clean fact in the bundle is the Ethics Commission's July 10 fine against Supervisor Shamann Walton, alongside a live package of campaign-finance and disclosure changes heading into committee. On the media side, the most relevant citywide stories were Mission Local's reporting on Grocery Outlet facial-recognition use and the public bank ballot measure, plus continued debate over street design, downtown recovery, and public safety.
