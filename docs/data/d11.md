*District 11 · Excelsior / Outer Mission / Ingleside*

## TLDR

- District 11 had a lighter week than Districts 9 and 10, but the Ocean and Mission corridors still have meaningful housing movement: 1601 Ocean Avenue is submitted, and 5630 Mission Street remains under review.
- The clearest direct neighborhood action is still a live Section 311 notice at 439 Bowdoin Street, open through July 23.

- Citywide policy fights still matter here because D11's near-term pipeline depends on the same financing and fee environment shaping projects everywhere else.

## Housing and Development

District 11's pipeline is smaller, but it is not empty. [1601 Ocean Avenue](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2025-010575PRJ) is the main live growth item, with 101 units proposed under AB 2011 plus a density bonus, while [5630 Mission Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2022-010009PRJ) remains under review as an affordable conversion-oriented project.

The older closed projects in the bundle, like [4840 Mission Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2016-012545PRJ) and [2340 San Jose Avenue](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2017-012151PRJ), are a reminder that District 11 has had sizable housing plans on the books for years. The real question is not whether the neighborhood can generate proposals; it is whether current projects move from filing into permits and construction faster than the city's usual dead zone.

## Permits and Small-Scale Change

The permit feed was almost entirely homeowner-scale work. The biggest items were ADU-related or interior expansion projects at `535 Brazil Avenue`, `1165 Holloway Avenue`, `227 Orizaba Avenue`, and `394 Naples Street`.

That pattern matters because it shows incremental densification and reinvestment, but not the kind of large multifamily momentum that would shift district housing totals quickly. District 11's visible change this week is mostly happening lot by lot.

## Citywide

The citywide calendar remains heavier than the district calendar. Land Use is moving planning-code changes that affect hotel uses, mezzanine rules, adaptive reuse, and large development agreements, while the full Board is also carrying inclusionary-housing fee changes and the drug-free supportive-housing ordinance.

Housing citywide still looks slow after approval. The build-timeline reference is useful here: a project can be "normal" even after sitting a year or more post-approval, but once timelines stretch into multiple years the risk of freeze or quiet death rises. That broader financing problem matters to D11 because the district's live corridor projects will be judged by the same market.

On ethics and money, the clearest headline is still the Ethics Commission fine against Supervisor Shamann Walton and a parallel round of campaign-disclosure work in committee. In journalism, the most relevant citywide threads were the public bank ballot story, the Great Highway measure qualifying for November, and continued reporting on neighborhood surveillance and public-space management.
