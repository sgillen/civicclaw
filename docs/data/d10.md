# SF Civic Digest — Week of July 13, 2026
*District 10 · Bayview / Hunters Point / Potrero Hill*

---

## TLDR

- Potrero Power Station is back at the center of District 10's agenda, with fresh planning notices and a Land Use hearing on amendments to the development agreement and special use district.
- The district's housing picture is mixed: 249 Pennsylvania just cleared with an approval letter for 124 affordable units, but the older 300 De Haro concept shows how easily large projects can be withdrawn or reshaped.
- Bayview has a live Board item on liquor-establishment relocation in the Third Street corridor, which could loosen how long-standing bar uses move within the district.
- Budget Committee also has two concrete D10 land items this week: a Bayview parcel purchase at `5000 Hunters Point Boulevard` and a PG&E easement deal under John McLaren Park.
- Permits were quieter here than in District 9, with the most visible activity being home repair or remodel work rather than major new construction.
- District 10 still has a solid action calendar, especially in Bayview and Potrero Hill, but the main civic story is land use and long-horizon development rather than cleanup volume.

## Housing and Development

District 10's biggest active land-use story is still Potrero Power Station. The project is back in committee with amendment requests, while the planning-notice layer shows that the city is reopening parts of the original framework rather than simply moving into quiet implementation.

The rest of the pipeline is mixed. [249 Pennsylvania Avenue](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2025-011975PRJ) just cleared with an approval letter for 124 affordable units, and [3275-3333 San Bruno Avenue](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2025-000470PRJ) remains under review. But [300 De Haro Street](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2020-006006PRJ) and an older [249 Pennsylvania Avenue](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim?search=2022-000613PRJ) concept were withdrawn, which is a reminder that not every large entitlement survives market conditions or project redesign.

## Permits and Neighborhood Change

The permit feed was relatively light and mostly incremental. The clearest items were fire-damage repair at `334 Wilde Avenue`, a residential remodel at `3836 San Bruno Avenue`, and an interior upgrade at `1515 19th Street`.

That is a different signal from a district where larger commercial tenants or new multifamily permits are starting to stack up. District 10's public story this week is still mostly about long-cycle development and land control, not a burst of near-term construction permits.

## Citywide

Citywide, the Board calendar is thick with planning code changes, campaign-finance rules, and homelessness policy. The same Land Use meeting touching Potrero Power Station also carries citywide housing code items, while the Board's Tuesday calendar includes inclusionary-housing fee changes and the drug-free supportive-housing ordinance.

Housing citywide is still caught in the same gap described in `sf-build-timelines.md`: approvals are easier to find than shovels in the ground. District 10 shows both sides of that clearly, with one large affordable approval, one long-running review, and multiple large concepts that were withdrawn or materially revised.

On ethics and money, the Walton fine is the sharpest item in the bundle, and it lands in the same week as committee work on campaign disclosures and consultant reporting. In local journalism, the most relevant district-facing items were Mission Local's reporting on Grocery Outlet facial-recognition use in Bayview and a new Black-owned bookstore planned for a shuttered Bayview storefront.
